Flight into Egypt

Escape by night, and weeping in Ramah

Egypt (Nile Delta) · 5–4 BC · Mt 2:13–18

Story

After the Magi had gone, the angel of the Lord appeared again to Joseph in a dream. Herod was searching for the child to kill him, so Joseph was to take the child and his mother and flee to Egypt. That very night Joseph rose and set out into the darkness.

Crossing the desert roads to the south, the family reached Egypt and stayed there until Herod died. Matthew records that this fulfilled the words of Hosea: 'Out of Egypt I called my son.'

When Herod realized the Magi had outwitted him, he flew into a rage. Reckoning from the time he had learned from them, he killed every boy two years old and under in Bethlehem and all its surroundings. In that wailing Matthew hears Rachel weeping for her children, refusing to be comforted.

Angel of the Lord · Mt 2:13

A voice is heard in Ramah, weeping and bitter mourning. Rachel weeps for her children and will not be comforted, because they are no more.

The prophecy of Jeremiah · Mt 2:18

The sayings are fresh renderings of the original meaning in today's language. Text: Mt 2:13–18

Background

Alexandria in Egypt had a large Jewish community at the time, making it a natural refuge. Bethlehem was a small town, so the children killed probably numbered a few dozen or fewer.

Pause and Reflect

When the violence of the world falls on the innocent, am I someone who stays beside their weeping?
