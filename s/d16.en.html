The Robe in the Cave at En-gedi

A hand in the cave that cut a robe instead of a throat

En-gedi · c. 1015 BC · 1 Sam 24:1–22

Story

Saul came with three thousand chosen men among the Rocks of the Wild Goats at En-gedi. Beside the sheepfolds along the road there was a cave. Saul went into it alone to relieve himself. Deep inside that very cave, in the darkness, David and his men sat holding their breath.

His men whispered to David, “This is the day the Lord told you about, when he would give your enemy into your hand.” David crept up without a sound and quietly cut off a corner of Saul's robe. But when he came back, his conscience struck him. David said to his men, “May the Lord keep me from laying a hand on my lord, the Lord's anointed.” With those words David held his men back from attacking Saul.

When Saul left the cave, David followed him out and called, “My lord the king!” David bowed with his face to the ground, held up the corner of the robe and said, “Look, my father. I cut off the corner of your robe and did not kill you.” Saul wept aloud and said, “Is that your voice, my son David? You are more righteous than I.” Saul went home, and David went up to the stronghold. A song from that cave remained: “In the shadow of your wings I take refuge.”

Background

En-gedi means “spring of the young goat.” It is an oasis where a spring bubbles up beneath the cliffs west of the Dead Sea, and Nubian ibex still live on its rocky heights. The limestone cliffs nearby are full of caves large and small.
