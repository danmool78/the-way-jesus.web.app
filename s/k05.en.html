A Heart Turned Away

The old king's heart turns away

High places facing the Mount of Olives · c. 935 BC · 1 Kgs 11:1–13

Story

A strange altar rose on the hill facing Jerusalem. It was a high place for Chemosh, the god of Moab, and beside it another went up for Molech, the god of the Ammonites. The man who built them was King Solomon, who had once asked for wisdom at Gibeon. His foreign wives burned incense and offered sacrifices there. The smoke climbed into the sky across from the temple.

Besides Pharaoh's daughter, Solomon loved women from Moab, Ammon, Edom, Sidon and the Hittites. These were the very nations of which the Lord had said, Do not marry them, for they will turn your hearts to other gods. He had seven hundred wives and three hundred concubines. As Solomon grew old, his wives turned the king's heart away. The king followed Ashtoreth, the goddess of the Sidonians, and Milcom, the god of the Ammonites.

The Lord was angry. He had appeared to Solomon twice. The Lord said to Solomon, “Since you have not kept my covenant, I will surely tear the kingdom away from you and give it to your servant. But for the sake of your father David, I will do it in your son's day. And I will not tear away the whole kingdom; for the sake of David and of Jerusalem, which I have chosen, I will leave one tribe to your son.”

Background

The southern summit of the Mount of Olives, east of Jerusalem, was later called the Mount of Corruption. The high places Solomon built there stood for some three hundred years, until King Josiah defiled them and tore them down.
