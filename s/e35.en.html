Ephphatha — Be Opened

The day closed ears and a bound tongue were set free

Decapolis · AD 29, summer · Mt 15:29–31 · Mk 7:31–37

Story

Jesus left Tyre, passed through Sidon, and took the long way around to the Sea of Galilee, toward the region of the Decapolis. People brought him a man who was deaf and could barely speak, and begged him to lay his hand on him. Jesus took him aside, away from the crowd, to a quiet place.

Jesus put his fingers into the man's ears, spat, and touched his tongue. Then he looked up to heaven, sighed deeply, and spoke a single word. Ephphatha. Be opened. At once the blocked ears were opened and the tangled tongue was loosed, and the man began to speak clearly.

Jesus urged them to tell no one, but the more he told them, the farther the news spread. When he went up on a mountain, people brought him the lame, the blind, and those who could not speak, and seeing them healed, the crowd gave glory to the God of Israel.

His Words

Jesus · Mk 7:34

Everything he does is wonderful. He makes the deaf hear and the mute speak.

The crowd · Mk 7:37

The sayings are fresh renderings of the original meaning in today's language. Text: Mt 15:29–31 · Mk 7:31–37

Background

Ephphatha is Aramaic, the everyday language of Judea in Jesus' time, and Mark often preserves such Aramaic expressions as they were spoken. The Decapolis was a region of ten Greek-style cities scattered east and south of the Sea of Galilee.
