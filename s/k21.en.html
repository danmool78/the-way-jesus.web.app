Micaiah Stands Alone

Four hundred against one

Samaria · c. 853 BC · 1 Kgs 22:1–40

Story

On the threshing floor by the gate of Samaria, two kings sat side by side in their royal robes. They were Ahab king of Israel and Jehoshaphat king of Judah. They were about to set out together to take Ramoth-gilead back from Aram. Four hundred prophets cried out with one voice, “Go up. The Lord will give the city into the king's hand.”

Jehoshaphat asked, “Is there no other prophet of the Lord here?” Ahab answered, “There is Micaiah son of Imlah. But I hate him. He never prophesies anything good about me.” The official sent to fetch Micaiah whispered to him, “Speak favorably, like the other prophets.” Micaiah answered, “I will say only what the Lord tells me.”

Micaiah said before the king, “I saw all Israel scattered on the hills like sheep without a shepherd.” The prophet Zedekiah struck Micaiah on the cheek, and Ahab had Micaiah thrown into prison. Ahab went into battle in disguise. But an arrow shot at random pierced Ahab's armor between its joints. That evening Ahab died, and when his chariot was washed at the pool of Samaria, dogs licked up his blood. It happened just as the Lord had said.

Background

Ramoth-gilead was a fortress town in Gilead, east of the Jordan, which Aram and Israel fought over many times. Jehoshaphat king of Judah married his son to Ahab's daughter Athaliah, so the two kings were joined by marriage.
