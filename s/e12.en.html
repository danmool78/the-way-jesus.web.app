Overturning the Temple Tables

His Father's house turned into a market

The Temple · Passover, AD 27 · Jn 2:13–25

Story

As Passover drew near, Jesus went up to Jerusalem. The Temple courts were packed with merchants selling cattle, sheep, and doves, and with money changers seated at their tables. The bellowing of animals and the clink of coins had crowded out the place of prayer.

He made a whip out of cords and drove out all the sheep and cattle, scattered the money changers' coins, and overturned their tables. To the dove sellers he said, do not turn my Father's house into a marketplace. His disciples remembered the psalm that says zeal for your house will consume me.

When asked what sign he would show, he answered: destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up. People scoffed that the Temple had taken forty-six years to build, but the temple he spoke of was his own body. Only after the resurrection did his disciples understand these words.

The sayings are fresh renderings of the original meaning in today's language. Text: Jn 2:13–25

Background

Herod began rebuilding the Temple around 20 BC. The money changers exchanged coins from many lands into Tyrian shekels for paying the Temple tax. John places this event early in Jesus' ministry.
