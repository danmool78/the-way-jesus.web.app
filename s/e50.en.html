Questions in the Temple Courts

Questions set as traps, answers that came back as love

The Temple · Holy Week · Tuesday · Mt 22:15–40 · Mk 12:13–44 · Lk 20:20–21:4

Story

Tuesday, and the Temple courts are taut with questions. Pharisees and Herodians hold out a single denarius. Is it right to pay taxes to Caesar? Whichever way he answered, the question was a trap. On the silver coin was the face of the emperor.

The Sadducees, who do not believe in the resurrection, bring the story of a woman married in turn to seven brothers. Then a teacher of the law asks, Which commandment is the first of all? The answer of Jesus was astonishingly simple. Love God, and love your neighbor.

When the arguing fades, Jesus sits opposite the offering box. After the rich have dropped in their heavy gifts and moved on, a poor widow quietly lets fall two small coins. His gaze lingers long on that faint sound.

The sayings are fresh renderings of the original meaning in today's language. Text: Mt 22:15–40 · Mk 12:13–44 · Lk 20:20–21:4

Background

The denarius of the time is thought to have carried the face of Emperor Tiberius and words honoring him as son of a god. The lepton the widow gave was the smallest coin in circulation.

Pause and Reflect

Do I give what is left over, or do I give myself?
