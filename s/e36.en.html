Who Do You Say I Am?

A question asked beneath the rock where the spring bursts out

Caesarea Philippi · AD 29, summer · Mt 16:13–28 · Mk 8:27–9:1 · Lk 9:18–27

Story

At the foot of Mount Hermon lies Caesarea Philippi, where the waters of the Jordan spring up from beneath the rock. In this unfamiliar land, lined with niches for foreign gods, Jesus asked his disciples as they walked along the road: Who do people say that I am?

Some say John the Baptist, others Elijah or Jeremiah, or one of the prophets. Then who do you say that I am? Peter answered: You are the Christ, the Son of the living God. Jesus called him blessed and gave him once again the name that means rock.

From then on, Jesus began to tell them that he must suffer many things in Jerusalem, be killed, and rise again on the third day. When Peter took hold of him and said this could never happen, Jesus rebuked him sharply. And he told all who wished to follow him to take up their own cross.

Background

Philip the tetrarch named the city in honor of the emperor; today it is Banias. The cave and temple ruins once devoted to the god Pan still remain, and one of the main sources of the Jordan River springs up here.
