Those Who Put Down Their Stones

The feast where he cried out for living water, and the words he wrote in the dust

The Temple · AD 29, autumn · Feast of Tabernacles · Jn 7:1–8:11

Story

It was the autumn Feast of Tabernacles, and Jerusalem was full of pilgrims and booths woven from branches. At a time when even his brothers did not believe in him, Jesus went up in secret, and halfway through the feast he began teaching in the Temple. On the last and greatest day of the feast, he stood and cried out: Anyone who is thirsty, come to me and drink.

The Temple guards sent to arrest him came back empty-handed and said: No one has ever spoken like this man. Early the next morning, the scribes and Pharisees dragged in a woman caught in adultery and made her stand in the middle. Moses told us to stone such a woman. What do you say?

Jesus bent down and wrote something on the ground with his finger. When they kept pressing him, he straightened up and said: Let the one among you who is without sin throw the first stone. One by one they slipped away, the oldest first, until only Jesus and the woman were left. Neither do I condemn you. Go.

Background

The story of the woman caught in adultery, John 7:53 to 8:11, is missing from the earliest manuscripts. But it is regarded as an ancient tradition and appears in most Bibles, often set off with brackets or a note.
