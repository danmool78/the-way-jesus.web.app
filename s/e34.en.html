A Mother from the Region of Tyre

A faith content with crumbs

Tyre · AD 29, summer · Mt 15:21–28 · Mk 7:24–30

Story

Jesus left Galilee and went north to the region of Tyre, a Gentile land by the sea. He entered a house and wanted no one to know he was there, but he could not stay hidden. A mother whose daughter was tormented by an evil spirit heard the news, came, and fell at his feet. She was not an Israelite.

Son of David, have mercy on me. She kept crying out, and the disciples, annoyed, urged him to send her away. Jesus' answer sounded cold. It is not right to take the children's bread and toss it to the dogs of the house. But the mother would not back down.

Yes, Lord. Yet even the dogs eat the crumbs that fall from their master's table. Jesus' face brightened. Your faith is great. When she went home, she found the child lying peacefully on the bed, and the spirit that had tormented her was already gone.

Jesus · Mk 7:27

Because you have answered this way, go home now. The evil spirit has already left your daughter.

Jesus · Mk 7:29

Yes, Lord. But even the dogs under the table eat the crumbs the children drop, do they not?

The mother · Mk 7:28

The sayings are fresh renderings of the original meaning in today's language. Text: Mt 15:21–28 · Mk 7:24–30

Background

Tyre was a Phoenician port city on the Mediterranean coast, Gentile territory in Jewish eyes. The word Jesus used refers not to wild dogs but to small dogs kept in the house.
