Nicodemus Comes by Night

The secret of the wind, heard in the dark

Jerusalem · Spring, AD 27 · Jn 3:1–21

Story

Nicodemus, a Pharisee and a leader of the Jews, came to Jesus at night. In a room lit by a single flickering lamp, he spoke carefully. No one could perform the signs you do, he said, unless God were with him.

Jesus answered that no one can see the kingdom of God without being born anew. When Nicodemus asked how an old man could be born again, Jesus spoke of being born of water and the Spirit, of the work of the Spirit that is like the wind, no one knowing where it comes from or where it goes.

Then came the words that, as Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, so the Son of Man must be lifted up. The confession that God so loved the world that he gave his only Son, and the words that light had come into the world but people loved the darkness more, lingered long in the stillness of the night.

The sayings are fresh renderings of the original meaning in today's language. Text: Jn 3:1–21

Background

The Greek word 'anothen', rendered 'anew', means both 'again' and 'from above'. Nicodemus appears again later at Jesus' burial, bringing myrrh and aloes.

Pause and Reflect

Am I still stuck in a faith that only dares to come to him carefully, under cover of night?
