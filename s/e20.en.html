Matthew at the Tax Booth

A man who rose from a place of scorn

Capernaum · AD 28 · Mt 9:9–13 · Mk 2:13–17 · Lk 5:27–32

Story

Jesus had gone out by the lake to teach the crowd, and as he walked along he came to the tax booth. Sitting there was Levi son of Alphaeus, also called Matthew, a tax collector scorned for siding with the powerful and collecting taxes from his own people.

Follow me. It was a single phrase. Levi got up, left everything behind, and followed him. Then he threw a great feast at his house, and many tax collectors and sinners reclined at the table with Jesus and his disciples.

The scribes of the Pharisees challenged the disciples. Why does your teacher eat and drink with people like these? Jesus heard and answered. The healthy do not need a doctor, the sick do. I came to call exactly such as these.

Background

Capernaum lay on the road where the Galilee of Herod Antipas met the territory of Philip, so it likely had a toll booth. Tax collectors often took more than was owed and were treated like sinners.
