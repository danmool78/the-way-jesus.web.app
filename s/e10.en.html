Come and See: The First Disciples

An invitation at four in the afternoon

Bethany beyond the Jordan · Early AD 27 · Jn 1:29–51

Story

At Bethany beyond the Jordan, John, who the day before had pointed to Jesus as the Lamb of God who carries away the sin of the world, was standing with two of his disciples when he saw him passing by and cried out again: look, the Lamb of God. At those words the two disciples followed him. Jesus turned and asked them what they were looking for.

When they asked where he was staying, he said, come and see. It was about four in the afternoon. One of the two who spent that day with him, Andrew, went to find his brother Simon and told him they had found the Messiah, and Jesus gave Simon a new name, Cephas.

The next day, on his way to Galilee, Jesus called Philip of Bethsaida. Philip ran to tell Nathanael. Nathanael scoffed that nothing good could come from Nazareth, but when Jesus said he had already seen him under the fig tree, his doubt gave way and he confessed his faith.

The sayings are fresh renderings of the original meaning in today's language. Text: Jn 1:29–51

Background

'The tenth hour' counts from sunrise in the Jewish way, so it means about four in the afternoon. 'Cephas' is Aramaic for rock; in Greek it is 'Peter'.

Pause and Reflect

When someone invites me to come and see, can I take the first step before my doubts do?
