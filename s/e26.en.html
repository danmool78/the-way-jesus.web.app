The Woman Who Wet His Feet with Tears

One who is forgiven much loves much

A Town in Galilee · AD 28 · Lk 7:36–50

Story

A Pharisee invited Jesus to a meal. The guests reclined around the table, leaning on one side with their feet stretched out behind them. Then a woman known in that town as a sinner came in carrying an alabaster jar of perfume and stood behind him at his feet.

The woman wept. As her tears fell on Jesus' feet, she wiped them with her hair, kissed his feet, and poured the perfume over them. Simon, the host, thought to himself: if this man were a prophet, he would know what sort of woman she is. Jesus began to tell him a story about two debtors.

Five hundred denarii and fifty denarii. If both were forgiven, which would love more? Turning toward the woman, Jesus asked Simon, Do you see this woman? Then he said to her, Your sins are forgiven. Your faith has saved you. Go in peace.

His Words

Her many sins have been forgiven, and that is why she has loved so greatly. One who is forgiven little loves little.

Jesus · Lk 7:47

Your faith has saved you. Go in peace.

Jesus · Lk 7:50

Do you see this woman? When I came into your house, you gave me no water for my feet, but she has wet my feet with her tears and dried them with her hair.

Jesus · Lk 7:44

The sayings are fresh renderings of the original meaning in today's language. Text: Lk 7:36–50

Background

Luke does not give this woman's name. Mary Magdalene is introduced separately in the very next scene, so there is no basis for treating them as the same person. That identification came from an interpretation in the Western church in the late sixth century.

Pause and Reflect

Do I know how great a forgiveness I have received, and is that knowing becoming the measure of my love?
