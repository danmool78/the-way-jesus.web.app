The Children and the Rich Young Man

A kingdom received like a child, and hands that would not let go

Perea beyond the Jordan · AD 30, spring · Mt 19:13–30 · Mk 10:13–31 · Lk 18:15–30

Story

In the region of Judea beyond the Jordan, people brought little children to Jesus for him to lay his hands on them, and the disciples scolded them. When Jesus saw it, he was indignant. Let the children come to me. Then he took them in his arms, laid his hands on them, and blessed them.

As he was setting out on his way, a man ran up and knelt before him. Good Teacher, what must I do to inherit eternal life? The man said he had kept all the commandments since his youth, and Jesus looked at him and loved him. You still lack one thing. Sell what you have, give to the poor, and come, follow me.

The man's face fell, and he went away grieving, for he had great wealth. Jesus said it is easier for a camel to go through the eye of a needle than for a rich person to enter the kingdom of God. To his astonished disciples Jesus said: With people this is impossible, but with God all things are possible.

The sayings are fresh renderings of the original meaning in today's language. Text: Mt 19:13–30 · Mk 10:13–31 · Lk 18:15–30

Background

After raising Lazarus, Jesus stayed for a while in Ephraim near the wilderness, then went to the region of Judea beyond the Jordan. Matthew calls the man young, Luke a ruler, Mark a man of great wealth, so he is often called the rich young ruler.
