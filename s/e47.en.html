The Perfume at Bethany

A broken jar, and the fragrance of love filling the house

Bethany · Six days before Passover · Mt 26:6–13 · Mk 14:3–9 · Jn 12:1–8

Story

On the evening six days before Passover, a dinner was held in Bethany, the hillside village east of Jerusalem. Lazarus, who had died and come back to life, sat at the table with the guests, and Martha bustled about serving the food. Mark tells us this dinner took place in the house of Simon, who had once had leprosy. Beneath the lamplight, the talk went back and forth in low voices.

Then Mary came in quietly, holding close an alabaster jar with a pound of costly pure nard. She broke the jar and poured the perfume over Jesus' head, then poured it on his feet too and wiped them with her loosened hair. The heavy fragrance filled the whole house. People held their breath, and no one dared to speak first.

This perfume could have been sold for three hundred denarii. Why wasn't it given to the poor? The sharp voice of Judas Iscariot broke the silence. He said this not because he cared about the poor, but because he kept the money bag and used to help himself to what was put in it. Jesus came to the woman's defense. Leave her alone. She has kept this for the day of my burial. The poor you will always have with you, but you will not always have me.

Background

Nard was a precious perfume drawn from the root of a plant that grows in the Himalayas. A day's wage was one denarius, so three hundred denarii was a fortune, nearly a full year of a laborer's work.
