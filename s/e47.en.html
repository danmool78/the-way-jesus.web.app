The Perfume at Bethany

A broken jar, and the fragrance of love filling the house

Bethany · Six days before Passover · Mt 26:6–13 · Mk 14:3–9 · Jn 12:1–8

Story

Evening, six days before Passover, in Bethany, a village on the hill east of Jerusalem. Lazarus, back from the dead, sits at the table with the guests, and Martha hurries back and forth with the food. Mark tells us the meal was held in the house of Simon, a man once sick with leprosy.

Then Mary comes in carrying costly perfume of pure nard. She breaks the alabaster jar and pours it on the head of Jesus, pours it on his feet too, and wipes them with her loosened hair. A heavy fragrance fills the room, and for a moment everyone holds their breath.

"That perfume could have been sold for three hundred denarii. Why wasn't the money given to the poor?" The sharp voice of Judas Iscariot breaks the silence. But Jesus shields the woman. In that fragrance he could already see his own burial drawing near.

Background

Nard was a precious perfume drawn from the root of a plant that grows in the Himalayas. A day's wage was one denarius, so three hundred denarii was a fortune, nearly a full year of a laborer's work.

Pause and Reflect

With a love that goes beyond counting the cost, what jar of mine might I break open before the Lord?
