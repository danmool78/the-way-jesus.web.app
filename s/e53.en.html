The Night in Gethsemane

Not my will, but yours

Gethsemane · Holy Week · Late Thursday night · Mt 26:36–56 · Mk 14:32–52 · Lk 22:39–53 · Jn 18:1–11

Story

After the supper, in the night, they cross the Kidron Valley and enter Gethsemane, a garden at the foot of the Mount of Olives. Moonlight filters through the olive trees. Jesus takes only Peter, James and John further in, and there he begins to tremble in deep anguish.

A little way off he falls to the ground and prays. Father, take this cup away from me. Yet not my will, but yours be done. His sweat falls to the ground like drops of blood, and each time he returns, the disciples have fallen asleep again.

A crowd with torches, swords and clubs pours into the garden. Judas comes up and kisses him. Peter swings a sword and cuts off the ear of Malchus, the high priest's servant, and Jesus touches the ear and heals it. Then all the disciples leave him and run.

The sayings are fresh renderings of the original meaning in today's language. Text: Mt 26:36–56 · Mk 14:32–52 · Lk 22:39–53 · Jn 18:1–11

Background

Gethsemane is usually explained as meaning olive press. It was an olive grove at the foot of the Mount of Olives, and Luke and John say Jesus often went there with his disciples.
