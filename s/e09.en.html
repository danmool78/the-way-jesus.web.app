Forty Days in the Wilderness

Three temptations overcome in a starving body

Judean Wilderness · AD 26, autumn to winter · Mt 4:1–11 · Mk 1:12–13 · Lk 4:1–13

Story

Before the water of baptism had even dried, the Spirit led Jesus into the wilderness. Mark notes that he was with the wild animals. After forty days and nights without food, when at last he felt hunger, the tempter came to him.

If you are the Son of God, the whisper went, turn these stones into bread. Then the tempter set him on the highest point of the Temple and told him to throw himself down, and on a high mountain he showed him all the kingdoms of the world and their glory, promising them all if he would bow down and worship.

Each time, Jesus answered with the words of Scripture. At last, at his firm command to be gone, the devil left, and angels came and served him. Luke adds that the devil departed for a time, waiting for another opportunity.

Background

The Judean wilderness is a dry stretch of limestone hills running from east of Jerusalem down to the Dead Sea. Matthew and Luke give the second and third temptations in a different order.
