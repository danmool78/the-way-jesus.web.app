By the Rivers of Babylon

The day the prison door opened

Babylon · c. 561 BC · 2 Kgs 25:22–30

Story

Only a few poor people were left in the ruined land of Judah, and the king of Babylon appointed Gedaliah, grandson of Shaphan, to govern them. When the army commanders who had scattered came to him at Mizpah, Gedaliah gave them his promise: “Do not be afraid of the Babylonians. Stay in the land and serve the king of Babylon, and all will go well with you.” But in the seventh month of that year, Ishmael, a man of the royal family, came with ten men and killed Gedaliah.

The people who were left were afraid that Babylon would take revenge for this, so they all fled down to Egypt. At that time, far away in a Babylonian prison, Jehoiachin king of Judah was locked up. He had become king at eighteen, and after only three months he had been carried off to Babylon. Thirty-seven years passed for him in prison that way.

It was the last month of the year that Evil-merodach became the new king of Babylon. The new king brought Jehoiachin out of prison, treated him kindly, and gave him a seat higher than those of the other kings held captive in Babylon. Jehoiachin took off his prison clothes, and for the rest of his life he ate at the king's table. The people carried off to Babylon sang this song: by the rivers of Babylon we sat and wept as we remembered Zion.

Background

Clay tablets found at the site of the palace in Babylon record rations of oil given to “Ya'ukin, king of Judah” and his sons. Jehoiachin had been carried from Jerusalem to Babylon in 597 BC.
