The Wedding at Cana

The first sign, water turned to wine

Cana · Early AD 27 · Jn 2:1–11

Story

A wedding feast was held in Cana of Galilee. The mother of Jesus was there, and Jesus and his disciples had been invited too. In the middle of the celebration, amid laughter and song, the wine ran out.

His mother came and told him they had no more wine. Even when he answered that his hour had not yet come, she told the servants to do whatever he said. Six large stone jars stood there, the kind used for Jewish purification rites.

The servants filled every jar to the brim with water and drew some out, and when the master of the feast tasted it, he called the bridegroom over. Everyone else serves the good wine first, he said, but you have saved the best until now. This was the first sign Jesus performed, and his disciples believed in him.

The sayings are fresh renderings of the original meaning in today's language. Text: Jn 2:1–11

Background

Wedding feasts then could last up to a week, and running out of wine was a great shame for the family. Each jar is thought to have held two or three metretes, roughly 80 to 120 liters.

Pause and Reflect

When something in my life runs dry, can I fill the jars with the faith that says, do whatever he tells you?
