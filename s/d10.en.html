Three Arrows

The arrow of promise that flew beyond

Gibeah (Saul's court) · c. 1018 BC · 1 Sam 20:1–42

Story

Fleeing from Naioth, David asked Jonathan, “What have I done, that your father is after my life?” Jonathan answered, “It can't be. You will not die.” David shook his head. “There is only a step between me and death.” David decided to stay away from the New Moon feast and hide in the field. As they made a covenant, Jonathan said, “Never withdraw your kindness from my house.”

On the second day of the New Moon, David's place at the king's table was empty again. Saul asked, “Why hasn't the son of Jesse come, either yesterday or today?” When Jonathan gave the excuse they had agreed on, Saul exploded: “As long as the son of Jesse lives, neither you nor your kingdom will be established. He must die.” Jonathan stood up to him: “Why should he die?” Instead of an answer, Saul's spear flew at his own son.

The next morning Jonathan went out to the field with a young servant boy. An arrow flew over the boy's head. Jonathan called out, “Isn't the arrow beyond you? Hurry! Don't stop!” It was the signal for David, hidden behind the rock, to go. When the boy had gone back, David came out and bowed down to the ground three times. The two kissed each other and wept together, but David wept the most. Jonathan said, “Go in peace.”

Background

The New Moon was the day the new moon appeared, a festival with trumpets, offerings and a shared meal (Numbers 10:10; 28:11). Scripture calls the rock beside the place where David hid and waited the stone Ezel.
