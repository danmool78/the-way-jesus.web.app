Talitha Koum

Beyond the news that it was too late

Capernaum · AD 28, autumn · Mt 9:18–26 · Mk 5:21–43 · Lk 8:40–56

Story

When Jesus crossed the lake again, Jairus, a leader of the synagogue, came running and fell at his feet. My little daughter is dying. Come and lay your hands on her. As Jesus went with him, a great crowd surrounded him and pressed against him. In that crush was a woman who had suffered from bleeding for twelve years.

She had spent all she had on many doctors and only grown worse. Believing that if she touched just his cloak she would be healed, she reached out from behind. In that instant the bleeding stopped. Who touched my clothes? To the trembling woman who came forward, Jesus said, Daughter, your faith has made you well.

Just then people came from the synagogue leader's house. Your daughter is dead. Jesus said, Do not be afraid, only believe, and he went into the room with only Peter, James, John, and the child's parents. He took her by the hand and said, Talitha koum, and the twelve year old girl got up and walked.

The sayings are fresh renderings of the original meaning in today's language. Text: Mt 9:18–26 · Mk 5:21–43 · Lk 8:40–56

Background

Talitha koum is Aramaic, the everyday language Jesus spoke. Mark preserves a few of Jesus' Aramaic words just as they sounded, such as Ephphatha, Abba, and Eloi, and then explains their meaning.

Pause and Reflect

When the news comes that it is already too late, can I hold on to the words, do not be afraid, only believe?
