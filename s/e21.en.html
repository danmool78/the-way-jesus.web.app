The Pool of Bethesda

The day thirty-eight years of waiting ended

Jerusalem · AD 28 · A Jewish festival · Jn 5:1–47

Story

A Jewish festival, at the pool of Bethesda beside the Sheep Gate in Jerusalem. In the shade of five colonnades lay crowds of the blind, the lame, and the withered. Among them was a man who had been ill for thirty-eight years.

Jesus asked him, Do you want to be well? Instead of answering, the man poured out his complaint. There was no one to put him in the pool when the water was stirred, and while he was making his way, someone else always went in first. Jesus told him to get up, pick up his mat, and walk, and at once he was healed and walked.

That day was the Sabbath. The Jewish leaders objected to a mat being carried on the Sabbath and to a healing on the Sabbath. When Jesus answered that his Father was still working and so he was working too, they sought all the more to kill him, for he was making himself equal with God.

Background

Excavations near the Church of Saint Anne in the late nineteenth century uncovered two large reservoirs. Some suggest that colonnades around the four sides and along the wall between the pools would match the five porches John describes.
