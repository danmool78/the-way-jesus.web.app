Night in Bethlehem

A night with no room, a song over the fields

Bethlehem · Winter, c. 5 BC · Mt 1:25 · Lk 2:1–20

Story

The Roman emperor Augustus ordered a census across the whole empire. Joseph left Nazareth with Mary, heavy with child, and walked the long road to Bethlehem, the town of his ancestor David. The unfamiliar town was already crowded with people.

There was no room for them to stay. That night Mary gave birth to her firstborn son, wrapped him in cloth, and laid him in a manger, the trough where animals feed. In the cold air, only the baby's faint breath rose like mist.

In the fields nearby, shepherds were keeping watch over their flocks through the night when suddenly the glory of the Lord blazed around them. The angel's news was followed by the praise of a heavenly army filling the night sky, and the shepherds hurried off and found the baby in the manger. Mary treasured all these things deep in her heart.

Background

The Greek word 'kataluma', usually rendered 'inn', can also mean the guest room of a family home. So some interpret Jesus as being born on the lower floor of a house where livestock were kept, or in a cave.

Pause and Reflect

If God chose the humblest of mangers, in what low place of my own life might I meet him?
