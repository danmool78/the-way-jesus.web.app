The Holy Bread of Nob and Goliath's Sword

Holy bread for a hungry fugitive, and what it cost

Nob · c. 1018 BC · 1 Sam 21:1–9; 22:6–23

Story

Having slipped away from Saul's court, David arrived empty-handed at the sanctuary of Nob. The priest Ahimelech came out trembling to meet him. “Why are you alone?” Ahimelech asked. “Is no one with you?” David made up a story: “The king has sent me on a secret errand. Just give me five loaves of bread.” There was nothing in the sanctuary but the holy bread that had just been removed. Ahimelech handed David the bread that had been replaced with fresh loaves before the Lord.

David asked again, “Is there a spear or a sword here? The king's business was so urgent I didn't even bring my sword.” Ahimelech answered, “The sword of Goliath, whom you struck down in the Valley of Elah, is here, wrapped in a cloth behind the ephod.” David said, “There is none like it. Give it to me.” But someone else was there that day: Doeg the Edomite, chief of Saul's shepherds. Doeg watched it all without a word.

Some time later Doeg reported to Saul what he had seen that day. At Saul's command, Doeg killed eighty-five priests. Only Abiathar, son of Ahimelech, escaped and ran to David. David said to Abiathar, “When I saw Doeg there that day, I knew this would happen. Your father's whole family is dead because of me. Stay with me. You are safe here.” Later David sang of Doeg, “Why do you boast of evil, you mighty man?”

Background

The bread of the Presence was twelve loaves set out fresh each Sabbath on the table in the sanctuary, and the loaves that were removed could be eaten only by priests (Lev 24:5–9). Jesus pointed to this event in a dispute about the Sabbath (Mark 2:25–26).
