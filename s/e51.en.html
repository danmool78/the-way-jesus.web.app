The Last Days, Seen from the Mount of Olives

Stones that will fall, and words that will never pass away

Mount of Olives · Holy Week · Tuesday evening · Mt 24:1–25:46 · Mk 13:1–37 · Lk 21:5–38

Story

As they leave the Temple, one of the disciples marvels. Teacher, look! What stones, what buildings! The great walls blazed in the sunlight. But Jesus says a day is coming when not one of these stones will be left standing.

At dusk, as Jesus sits on the Mount of Olives facing the Temple, Peter, James, John and Andrew ask him quietly. When will these things happen? He tells them of wars and earthquakes and famines, of deception and persecution, and of those who endure to the end.

No one knows that day or hour, he says again and again, so stay awake. The stories of the bridesmaids with their lamps ready, the servants who made good on the talents entrusted to them, and the people who cared for the least drift low over the darkening city.

Background

The Temple, greatly expanded by Herod, was burned and destroyed in AD 70 by the Roman army under Titus. The Western Wall in Jerusalem today is part of the retaining wall that held up the Temple Mount.

Pause and Reflect

As I wait for the one whose coming no one knows, how am I staying awake today?
