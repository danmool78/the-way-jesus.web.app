The Good Samaritan and Two Sisters

One who becomes a neighbor, one who sits at his feet

Bethany · AD 29, autumn · Lk 10:25–42

Story

A teacher of the law stood up to test Jesus. Teacher, what must I do to inherit eternal life? Asked in return what was written in the law, he recited that one must love God with all one's heart and soul and strength and mind, and love one's neighbor as oneself. Your answer is right. Do this, and you will live. Wanting to justify himself, he asked again. And who is my neighbor?

Jesus told a story. A man going down from Jerusalem to Jericho fell among robbers, who stripped him, beat him, and left him half dead. A priest happened to be going down that road, and when he saw him, he passed by on the other side. A Levite did the same. But a Samaritan traveling that way saw him and was moved with pity. He went to him, poured oil and wine on his wounds and bandaged them, then put him on his own animal, brought him to an inn, and took care of him. Which one became a neighbor to that man? The one who showed him mercy, the teacher of the law answered. Go and do the same.

As they traveled on, Jesus came into a village, and a woman named Martha welcomed him into her home. Martha was rushing about with all the serving, while her sister Mary sat at the Lord's feet and listened to his words. At last Martha could bear it no longer and came to him. Lord, don't you care that my sister has left me to do the work alone? Martha, Martha. You are anxious and troubled about many things. But only one thing is needed. Mary has chosen the good part, and it will not be taken from her.

Background

From Jerusalem to Jericho was about twenty-seven kilometers, a wilderness descent of more than a thousand meters. Luke says only 'a certain village,' but John's Gospel identifies the village of Martha and Mary as Bethany.
