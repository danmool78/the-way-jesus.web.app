The Man Among the Tombs

A darkness stronger than chains, a word stronger still

Gerasa (Kursi) · AD 28, autumn · Mt 8:28–34 · Mk 5:1–20 · Lk 8:26–39

Story

Across the lake, in the country of the Gerasenes. As Jesus stepped out of the boat, a man who lived among the tombs came running. Not even iron chains could hold him. Night and day, among the tombs and in the hills, he cried out and cut himself with stones.

He fell down before Jesus and shouted, Jesus, Son of the Most High God, do not torment me. When asked his name, he answered, Legion, for we are many. The unclean spirits entered a herd of about two thousand pigs, which rushed down the steep bank into the sea.

People came running and found him sitting there, clothed and in his right mind, and they were afraid, and begged Jesus to leave. When the man asked to go with him, Jesus told him to go home and tell what the Lord had done. And he spread the news all through the Decapolis.

Background

Mark and Luke call it the region of the Gerasenes, Matthew the region of the Gadarenes, and the manuscripts disagree on the name. At Kursi on the eastern shore stand the remains of a fifth century Byzantine monastery commemorating this event.

Pause and Reflect

If Jesus sent me home, what would I tell those closest to me about what he has done for me?
