A House of Prayer

Overturned tables, a courtyard opened again for prayer

The Temple · Holy Week · Monday · Mt 21:12–17 · Mk 11:12–19 · Lk 19:45–48

Story

Monday morning, on the road climbing from Bethany to Jerusalem. Hungry, Jesus stops before a fig tree thick with leaves. There is no fruit on any branch. He speaks to the tree, and the disciples take note of his words.

The outer court of the Temple was like a marketplace. Coins jingling, doves beating their wings, the shouts of bargaining. Jesus drives out the buyers and sellers and overturns the tables of the money changers and the seats of those selling doves. He lets no one carry goods across the Temple courts.

Where the uproar dies down, the blind and the lame come to him and are healed. Children shout, "Hosanna to the Son of David!" The chief priests look for a way to get rid of him, but the people hang on his every word.

His Words

Jesus · Mk 11:17

Yes, I hear them. Have you never read, From the lips of children and infants you have brought praise to perfection?

Jesus · Mt 21:16

From now on and forever, may no one ever eat fruit from you again.

Jesus · Mk 11:14

The sayings are fresh renderings of the original meaning in today's language. Text: Mt 21:12–17 · Mk 11:12–19 · Lk 19:45–48

Background

The outer court of the Temple was the Court of the Gentiles, open even to non-Jews. Pilgrims bought their sacrifices here, and are known to have exchanged money for Tyrian shekels to pay the Temple tax.

Pause and Reflect

Is the temple of my heart a house of prayer, or a busy market full of trading?
