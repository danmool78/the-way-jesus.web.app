Who Is the Greatest?

He sets a child in the middle of them

Capernaum · AD 29, autumn · Mt 18:1–35 · Mk 9:33–37 · Lk 9:46–48

Story

On the way back to Capernaum, the disciples lagged behind, arguing in low voices. When they entered the house, Jesus asked: What were you arguing about on the road? No one answered. Who was the greatest, that had been their quarrel.

Jesus sat down, called the Twelve, took a little child, and stood the child in the middle of them. Then he took the child in his arms. Whoever wants to be first must be last of all and servant of all. Whoever welcomes one such child in my name welcomes me.

Jesus told of a shepherd who leaves the ninety-nine to go looking for the one lost sheep. Peter asked: If my brother wrongs me, how many times must I forgive him? Is seven times enough? Jesus told him to forgive even seventy-seven times, and told the parable of a servant who was forgiven a huge debt yet would not forgive a small one.

The sayings are fresh renderings of the original meaning in today's language. Text: Mt 18:1–35 · Mk 9:33–37 · Lk 9:46–48

Background

In the parable, the servant was forgiven ten thousand talents, while his fellow servant owed him a hundred denarii. One talent was about six thousand days' wages for a laborer, an almost unimaginable contrast.

Pause and Reflect

Do I forgive others while remembering how much I have been forgiven?
