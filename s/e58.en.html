Through the Locked Door

Peace that came through a locked door

The Upper Room · That night, and eight days later · Lk 24:36–49 · Jn 20:19–29

Story

On the evening of the resurrection, the disciples are gathered behind doors locked for fear. Just as the room is stirring with the story of the two who came back from Emmaus, Jesus stands among them. Peace be with you. Startled, the disciples think they are seeing a ghost.

He shows them his hands and feet with the marks of the nails, and his side pierced by the spear. He eats a piece of broiled fish in front of them, opens their minds to understand the scriptures, and tells them that the Christ had to suffer and rise, and that the news of forgiveness would go out to every nation. Then he breathes on them and says, Receive the Holy Spirit.

Thomas, who was not there, holds out. Unless he touches for himself, he will not believe. Eight days later, behind the locked door again, Jesus stands and holds out his hands and side to him. A confession bursts from Thomas. My Lord and my God.

The sayings are fresh renderings of the original meaning in today's language. Text: Lk 24:36–49 · Jn 20:19–29

Background

Thomas was called Didymus, meaning the Twin. In Jewish reckoning, eight days later counts the first day too, so it points to the same day of the following week, the first day of the week come round again.

Pause and Reflect

Behind the door of my heart that I have locked in fear, am I letting his peace come in?
