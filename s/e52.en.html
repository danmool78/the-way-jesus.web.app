The Last Supper in the Upper Room

The last night, when he washed their feet and broke the bread

The Upper Room · Holy Week · Thursday night · Mt 26:17–30 · Mk 14:12–26 · Lk 22:7–38 · Jn 13:1–17:26

Story

Thursday evening. Peter and John follow a man carrying a water jar and prepare the Passover table in a large upper room. When the disciples have gathered in the lamplight, Jesus takes off his outer robe, ties a towel around his waist, pours water into a basin, and washes their feet one by one.

During the meal he takes bread, gives thanks, breaks it, and gives it to them, saying, This is my body, given for you. Passing the cup, he says, This is my covenant blood, poured out so that the sins of many may be forgiven. At the words, One of you will betray me, the room goes cold, and Judas gets up and goes out. It was night.

To the disciples who remain, Jesus speaks for a long time. Love one another, he says. Do not let your hearts be troubled. I am the way. Then he looks up to heaven and prays for them. After singing a hymn, they go out into the dark toward the Mount of Olives.

Jesus · Jn 14:27

The sayings are fresh renderings of the original meaning in today's language. Text: Mt 26:17–30 · Mk 14:12–26 · Lk 22:7–38 · Jn 13:1–17:26

Background

Matthew, Mark and Luke present this supper as a Passover meal, while John says Jesus was crucified on the day of preparation for Passover. Scholars still discuss how the two accounts fit together.

Pause and Reflect

In the light of his last request to love one another, whose feet should I be the first to wash?
