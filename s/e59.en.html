Morning on the Shore of Tiberias

Love, asked three times beside a charcoal fire

Shore of the Sea of Tiberias · A morning after the resurrection · Jn 21:1–25

Story

Back in Galilee, Peter says, I am going fishing. Thomas and Nathanael, the sons of Zebedee and two other disciples get into the boat with him. All night they cast the net and catch nothing. As day breaks, someone is standing on the shore.

When they do as he says and cast the net on the right side of the boat, so many fish fill it that they cannot haul it in. The beloved disciple cries out, It is the Lord! Peter wraps his cloak around him and jumps into the water. On the shore, fish and bread lie on a charcoal fire, and in the net are a hundred and fifty-three large fish.

After breakfast, Jesus asks Peter three times, Simon son of John, do you love me? At the third question Peter is hurt. Lord, you know everything; you know that I love you. Feed my sheep. And follow me.

The sayings are fresh renderings of the original meaning in today's language. Text: Jn 21:1–25

Background

In John's Gospel the word for a charcoal fire appears only twice: in the courtyard of the high priest's house where Peter denied Jesus, and on this morning. Three denials are followed by three confessions of love.
