Lazarus, Come Out

The one who wept at the tomb and called death to give way

Bethany · AD 30, early in the year · Jn 11:1–53

Story

Word came from Bethany. Lord, the one you love, Lazarus, is sick. Yet Jesus stayed where he was two more days before setting out. When he said they would go back to Judea, Thomas said: Let us also go, that we may die with him.

By the time they arrived, Lazarus had already been in the tomb four days. Martha ran out and said: Lord, if you had been here, my brother would not have died. I am the resurrection and the life. Do you believe this? Then Mary came and fell at his feet weeping, and Jesus wept too.

Before the cave tomb sealed with a stone, Jesus told them to take the stone away. Despite Martha's worry that there would be a smell, the stone was rolled aside. Jesus looked up to heaven and gave thanks, then cried out in a loud voice: Lazarus, come out. And the man who had died walked out, his hands and feet wrapped in grave cloths. After this, the high priest Caiaphas told the council it was better for one man to die for the people. John recorded those words as a prophecy that Jesus would die for the nation, and to gather into one the scattered children of God.

The sayings are fresh renderings of the original meaning in today's language. Text: Jn 11:1–53

Background

John writes that Bethany was fifteen stadia, about three kilometers, from Jerusalem. Later Jewish writings reflect the idea that the soul of the dead lingers near the body for three days, so four days was a time when hope had run out.

Pause and Reflect

Even in grief, when the Lord seems to have come too late, do I believe in him who is the resurrection and the life?
