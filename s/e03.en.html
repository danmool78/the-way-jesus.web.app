Simeon and Anna in the Temple

A light in the eyes of two who waited a lifetime

The Temple · 40 days after the birth · Lk 2:21–38

Story

On the eighth day the child was circumcised and named Jesus, as the angel had said. When the days of purification were complete, Joseph and Mary carried him up to the Temple in Jerusalem and, as the Law required, offered a pair of turtledoves or two young pigeons.

Just then Simeon, a righteous and devout old man, was led by the Spirit into the Temple. He had lived with a promise that he would not die before he saw the Christ. He took the child in his arms and praised God, then told Mary that a day would come when sorrow would pierce her heart like a sword.

Anna, too, came near, the daughter of Phanuel of the tribe of Asher, a widow of eighty-four. She was a prophet who never left the Temple, fasting and praying night and day. She gave thanks to God and spoke about the child to everyone who was waiting for the redemption of Jerusalem.

The sayings are fresh renderings of the original meaning in today's language. Text: Lk 2:21–38

Background

Leviticus 12 allows a family of limited means to offer two birds instead of a lamb. That Joseph and Mary offered birds shows that this was a poor family.
