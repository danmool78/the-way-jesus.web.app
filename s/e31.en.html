A Feast in the Wilderness

Five barley loaves and two fish

Fields of Bethsaida · AD 29, spring · Near Passover · Mt 14:13–21 · Mk 6:30–44 · Lk 9:10–17 · Jn 6:1–15

Story

The disciples had come back from their mission worn out. They crossed by boat to a quiet place near Bethsaida to rest a while, but people had heard and hurried ahead along the shore. Jesus had compassion on them, for they were like sheep without a shepherd, and he taught them until the sun was low.

You give them something to eat. The disciples were at a loss. Philip reckoned that two hundred denarii worth of bread would not be enough, and all Andrew could find was a boy's five barley loaves and two fish. It was spring, with Passover near, and the people sat down in groups on the green grass.

Jesus looked up to heaven, gave thanks, broke the bread, and shared it, and the food passed from hand to hand without end. Five thousand men ate their fill, and the leftover pieces filled twelve baskets. When the people tried to seize him and make him king, Jesus withdrew to the mountain alone.

The sayings are fresh renderings of the original meaning in today's language. Text: Mt 14:13–21 · Mk 6:30–44 · Lk 9:10–17 · Jn 6:1–15

Background

It is the only miracle recorded in all four Gospels. Luke names the place as Bethsaida, thought to be near the mouth of the Jordan on the northeast shore of the Sea of Galilee. John notes that the boy's loaves were barley bread, the food of the poor.
