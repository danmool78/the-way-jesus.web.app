Silence at the Trial

A silent king, and a denial spoken three times

The Praetorium · Holy Week · Early Friday morning · Mt 26:57–27:26 · Mk 14:53–15:15 · Lk 22:54–23:25 · Jn 18:12–19:16

Story

Bound, Jesus is led first to Annas, then to Caiaphas the high priest. While false testimony contradicts itself, he stays silent. But when asked, Are you the Christ, he answers, I am. The high priest tears his robes, and the council cries out for death.

At that same hour, warming himself by a charcoal fire in the courtyard, Peter denies three times that he knows him. A rooster crows, and the Lord turns and looks at him. Peter goes outside and weeps bitterly.

At dawn the council hands Jesus over to Pilate the governor. Finding no crime, Pilate even sends him to Herod, but the crowd only shouts, Crucify him. In the end Pilate washes his hands, releases Barabbas, has Jesus flogged, and hands him over.

Background

Pontius Pilate was the Roman governor of Judea from AD 26 to 36. In 1961 a stone inscription bearing his name was found at Caesarea on the Mediterranean coast.

Pause and Reflect

Before the truth, do I stand with the silent Jesus, or let myself be carried along by the shouting crowd?
