Joash, the Child Hidden in the Temple

David's lamp, almost put out

Jerusalem · c. 835 BC · 2 Kgs 11:1–21

Story

When Ahaziah king of Judah died, his mother Athaliah rose up and killed the whole royal family. On the day the sword swept through the palace, Ahaziah's sister Jehosheba secretly took her baby nephew Joash and hid him with his nurse in a bedroom. For the next six years the child grew up hidden in the temple of the Lord, while Athaliah ruled the land.

In the seventh year, the priest Jehoiada called the commanders of hundreds to the temple, put them under oath and showed them the king's son. Jehoiada handed out to them the spears and shields that had belonged to King David. The guards stood with weapons in hand along both sides of the temple. Jehoiada brought out the boy, put the crown on him and anointed him. The people clapped their hands and shouted, “Long live the king!”

Athaliah heard the shouting and came into the temple. When she saw the young king standing by the pillar and the people blowing trumpets, Athaliah tore her clothes and screamed, “Treason! Treason!” Jehoiada gave the order: “Do not put her to death inside the temple.” Athaliah was dragged out and put to death, and the people tore down the temple of Baal. Seven-year-old Joash sat on the throne of David.

Background

Second Chronicles tells us that Jehosheba was the wife of Jehoiada the priest. Athaliah came from Ahab's house in northern Israel and married into Judah, and she brought Baal worship into Jerusalem.
