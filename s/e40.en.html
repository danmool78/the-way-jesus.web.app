Eyes Opened at Siloam

Eyes opened with mud and pool water

Pool of Siloam · AD 29, autumn · Jn 9:1–41

Story

Passing through the streets of Jerusalem, Jesus saw a man who had been blind from birth. His disciples asked: Whose sin made this man blind, his own or his parents'? Jesus said it was neither. It happened so that the works of God might be revealed in him.

Jesus spat on the ground, made mud, spread it on the man's eyes, and told him to go and wash in the Pool of Siloam. Groping his way, the man went down the steps and washed his face in the water. And he came back seeing light for the first time in his life. That day was the Sabbath.

The Pharisees questioned him again and again, and even his parents, afraid, held their tongues. He held firm to the end. One thing I know: I was blind, and now I see. After he was driven out of the synagogue, Jesus came looking for him, and he fell down, saying, Lord, I believe.

Background

In 2004, during sewer work in the south of Jerusalem, a stepped pool from the Second Temple period was discovered, and it is thought to be the Pool of Siloam in John's Gospel. Siloam means 'sent.'
